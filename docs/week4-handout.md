## Part 1 · Two practices (Experiments 1 and 3) — about 10 minutes
In **Experiment → Guided run**, choose **Meditation practices**, pick the **order** (your choice — and say why in your notebook), and press **▶ Start guided run**. The app runs:

| Step | What happens | Length |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A teammate **reads the script aloud**, then the **eyes-closed rest** starts | 1 min |
| 2 | Read script → **first practice** → **ratings** | 3 min |
| 3 | Read script → **second practice** → **ratings** | 3 min |

**Readers:** speak slowly and quietly. When you finish, stay silent — the block starts by itself (or press the button when the participant is ready). During a block, watch the big bar at the top; don't talk.

**Ratings (Experiment 3):** right after each practice, the participant answers four quick questions, 1–5:
**Stability** (how consistently did you maintain your intended attention?) · **Effort** (how much effort was required?) · **Meta-awareness** (how aware were you of what your mind was doing?) · **Sleepiness**. Answer straight away, about the practice you just did.

### The scripts (the app shows them too)
**Focused attention** — Close your eyes gently and relax, as you would when preparing to sleep. Keep your attention alert; don't strain your eyes, cross them, or look upward. Rest your inner gaze about eight to ten inches ahead, into the field of darkness in front of you. Silently repeat any calming word. Stay still, and watch your inner experience calmly, as though images were arising on a screen. If you get distracted, gently return to the word and the dark space in front of you.

**Open monitoring** — Close your eyes gently and settle into a comfortable, still posture. Rest your attention in an open, relaxed way, without choosing any one thing to focus on. Notice whatever arises as it comes: sounds, body sensations, thoughts, feelings, images. Let each experience appear and pass, without following it, judging it, or trying to change it. If you notice you have been carried off by a thought, just notice that, and return to open, wide awareness. Stay alert and curious, like watching clouds cross a wide sky.

*(These scripts follow the standard definitions — focused attention: sustained attention on a chosen object; open monitoring: non-reactive monitoring of whatever arises (Lutz et al., 2008). They are class scripts, not protocols copied from a specific study.)*

### Read the results
Scroll to **Practice results**:
- **alpha above 1/f (ears)** and **theta above 1/f (forehead)** — “is there a bump above the smooth background?” 0 dB means no bump. (Raw band power can mislead when the low-frequency background changes.)
- Your four ratings for each practice, and the **rest** row as your reference.

Questions for your notebook:
1. Did the two practices differ in alpha or theta? By how much compared with rest?
2. Does the practice with the **highest alpha** also have the highest **stability**? *Perhaps, perhaps not.* With one person and two practices this is a description, not a test — what would you need to say more?
3. Was **sleepiness** different between the practices? Could drowsiness explain an alpha or theta difference?
4. The order was your choice. Could order alone explain what you see?

## Part 2 · Can we observe mind wandering? (Experiment 2) — about 6 minutes
In **Guided run**, choose **Mind wandering: focused attention + key presses** and start. After the script, the participant does **focused attention for 4 minutes** and presses the **SPACEBAR** every time they **notice** their mind has wandered, then gently returns to the word. Keep one finger on the spacebar. (There is also an on-screen button.) The cue bar counts the presses.

### Read the results
Scroll to **Mind-wandering windows**. For each press the app cuts out **−10 s → press → +10 s**:
- **Grey lines** are individual presses; the **coloured line** is their average; the **grey band** is what the *same analysis gives at random times in the same block* (95% range).
- If the coloured line stays inside the band, the presses look no different from chance.
- Below that is a small **spectrogram for each press** (red line = the press).
- The **head movement** panel shows whether the participant moved when they pressed.

Notebook questions:
1. Does EEG activity change around the moment of the press? Does anything leave the grey band — and where (before 0, at 0, after)?
2. Is any change at 0 s just the **movement of pressing**? Check the head-movement panel.
3. The press marks when the participant *noticed* the wandering — **not when it began**. What does that mean for the −10 s side?
4. How many presses were usable? What would more presses, or a longer block, change?
5. This is a classroom adaptation of a study by Hasenkamp et al. (2012), who used fMRI and experienced meditators. What is different here?

## Troubleshooting
| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| The guided run says ear contact is poor | Fix hair behind the ears / dampen the sensors, or press **Start anyway** |
| Spacebar does nothing | The press block must be running (cue bar shows the block). Click on the page background first, not a menu |
| No ratings screen | It appears after each practice; answer all four to enable **Continue** |
| “No clear peak” / no results | Block too short, or poor contact. Rest and the practices need their full length |
| Everything is slow | Close other tabs; keep this tab visible |
| Nothing works | **Practice** mode (no headset) runs the same sequences |
