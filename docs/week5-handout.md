# PSYC 20N · Lab 5 — Can five minutes change your attention?

**Open this link (Chrome):** https://bdl.stanford.edu/muse-web/?week=5
**This experiment needs no headset.** Use the **keyboard**. Work in pairs or threes: one person is the participant; teammates watch the screen, keep time, and stay quiet during the intervention. Everything runs in your browser — nothing is uploaded.

## The design
**Arrow task → 5-minute intervention → arrow task again.** In the **arrow (Flanker) task**, five arrows appear; respond only to the **middle** arrow (← or F if it points left, → or J if it points right). Sometimes the other arrows point the same way (*congruent*), sometimes the opposite way (*incongruent*). Incongruent trials are slower — that difference is the **flanker effect**, a rough measure of how well you ignore distractions.

Choose **one** intervention (your group decides — say why in your notebook):
- **Focused-attention meditation** (a teammate reads the script aloud, then stays silent)
- **Physical exercise** (light-to-moderate, comfortable, optional — stop if you feel unwell)
- **Stroop cognitive training** (name the *ink* colour of colour words)
- **Quiet rest**

## Before you start — write your predictions
1. Will your reaction time (RT) get faster, slower, or stay the same after the intervention? Why?
2. Will the **flanker effect** (incongruent − congruent RT) shrink, grow, or stay the same?
3. What else could change your RT besides the intervention? (Think: practice, tiredness, boredom, mood.)

## Run it (about 12 minutes)
1. Enter the participant's **initials**, choose the **intervention**, press **▶ Start experiment** (top right).
2. **Practice** (8 trials, with ✓/✗ feedback) → **Baseline arrow task** (60 trials, ~2 min).
3. **Intervention (5 minutes).** Follow the on-screen instructions. *Teammates:* watch the timer; tell the participant when time is up (the screen will say so; there is an optional beep checkbox, off by default so other groups aren't disturbed).
4. **Arrow task again** (60 trials), then your **results** appear.
5. **Download data (.zip)** and **Save results card (.png)** — we will compare groups on Tuesday.

You can **Quit** at any time; the data so far are kept and can be downloaded.

## Read your results
You get three **summary cards** — **Flanker interference**, **overall reaction time**, and **accuracy** — each showing baseline → after → change, plus a note on whether the change is bigger than ordinary trial-to-trial noise. Below are two charts (reaction time by trial type; flanker interference with error bars) and an **All metrics** table: mean, median and variability (SD) of RT, accuracy for congruent and incongruent trials, and counts of correct, incorrect and unanswered trials. RT statistics use **correct trials only**.

Questions for your notebook (they also appear on screen under **Think about it**):
1. **Speed or accuracy?** Did RT and accuracy move together? Faster but less accurate is a *speed–accuracy trade-off*, not a clear improvement.
2. **Practice or intervention?** You did the task twice. How much of any change could be practice, boredom or tiredness? How would other groups' results — especially quiet rest — help you tell?
3. **How far can you go?** What would you need (control group, many participants, a delay before retesting, a different task) to say the intervention *caused* the change?

**An immediate change after five minutes does not show lasting attention training or transfer to other tasks.** It is one person and 60 trials per test.
