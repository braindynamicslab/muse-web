# PSYC 20N · Lab 3 — The brainwave dictionary on trial

**Open this link (Chrome):** https://bdl.stanford.edu/muse-web/?week=3
Practice mode (no headset) works too. **One laptop per group** connects to the headband; teammates watch the big colored cue at the top of the page and tell the wearer when to switch. Rotate who wears it — everyone gets recorded for Experiments 1 and 2.

---

## Before you start (every wearer)
1. Wipe the sensors, let them dry. Hair away from the forehead sensors; ear sensors tucked behind the ears **against skin**, slightly damp.
2. Click **Connect Muse**. Wait until **all four dots are green** — especially **TP9 and TP10** (the ears). Alpha is strongest there. If a dot is yellow/red: move hair, snug the band, wet the ear sensor, relax your jaw, hold still. Hover over a dot to see why.
3. Sit still, jaw relaxed.

---

## Experiment 1 · Find your alpha *(everyone, ~3 min each)*
In **Experiment → Guided run**, choose **Find your alpha** and press **▶ Start guided run**. After a 5-second countdown the app runs three 45-second blocks by itself:

**Eyes open (1) → Eyes closed → Eyes open (2)**

- Eyes open: look at one fixed spot. Eyes closed: relax — don't squeeze. Same posture and jaw the whole time.
- Teammates: watch the cue at the top. When it changes, say **“open”** or **“close”** out loud, quietly.
- Don't make this a research recording — it's your own classroom observation.

When it finishes, scroll to **Spectrum lab** (results row). Choose the **Eyes closed** block, view **Compare channels**:

1. Did alpha power (8–13 Hz) go **up** with eyes closed? (Compare it with the open blocks in the table and the Compare chart.)
2. Where is your alpha peak? Write it down: **IAF = ______ Hz**
3. Is **TP9 similar to TP10**? (Same peak frequency? Similar height?)
4. Did alpha come back down in the second eyes-open block?

*“No clear peak”? That is a real result.* Some people have a small or no alpha peak, and a poor ear contact can hide one. Note what you saw and move on.

---

## Experiment 2 · Does the reference change the answer? *(everyone, ~8 min, same data)*
Take the same eyes-closed recording. In **Spectrum lab**, set **View → Compare references** and pick an electrode (try TP9). The same data is re-expressed four ways:

- **Device reference** — as the Muse records it
- **Average of all four** — each channel minus the mean of all four
- **Linked ears** — minus the mean of TP9 and TP10
- **Bipolar** — minus the electrode on the other side (TP9 − TP10)

Answer:
1. Did the **brain** change? (No — only how we measure it.)
2. Did the **spectrum** change? Did **alpha power** change? By how many dB?
3. Did **IAF** (the peak position) change?
4. Which EEG features are robust to how we measure them, and which are not?

*Caution:* with only four electrodes an “average reference” is a rough approximation, and a noisy channel leaks into the others.

## Experiment 3 · Put a claim on trial *(your group, ~20 min)*
Pick **one claim** (write your first and second choice on the board; two groups may pick the same one — that's a replication!):

| Claim | Your A / B conditions |
|---|---|
| **Attention** — “Beta = busy mind” | Quiet rest vs **silent** mental arithmetic |
| **Working memory** — “Theta = memory” | Hold 1–2 digits vs 6–7 digits |
| **Meditation** — “Alpha = relaxed” | Eyes-closed rest vs meditation |
| **Mind wandering** | Focused attention (count breaths) vs mind wandering |
| **Gamma / muscle** — “Gamma = concentration” | Quiet rest vs arithmetic vs **jaw clench** |
| **Design your own** | Your own conditions |

**Steps**
1. Choose your claim in the **Experiment** box. Read the tip.
2. **Write your prediction first:** which feature changes, in which direction. (“beta goes up in arithmetic vs rest.”) *Decide before you look.*
3. Run **A–B–A–B**: **Guided run → A–B–A–B**, pick your two conditions, 45 s blocks, ×2. Alternating protects you from drift and order effects (e.g. getting sleepy over time).
4. In **Compare two conditions** (A = reference), read the result. The app tells you whether your prediction matched.
5. Click **Save result card (.png)** and bring it to the debrief.

**Design rules:** same posture every block · silent tasks (talking = jaw noise) · eyes open and fixed unless eyes-closed is the point · note anything odd (itch, sneeze, lost count) with the time.

**Gamma group:** switch the spectrogram to **1–80 Hz** (it switches automatically for your claim). Watch the **muscle ratio** under the EEG traces and the 40–80 Hz region (the 60 Hz power-line band is greyed out). What does a jaw clench do — and what does that mean for “gamma” measured at the scalp with a Muse?

**Working-memory group:** show digits on a timer (slide or PsyToolkit in a split tab), then hold them silently. Mark the retention periods as your blocks. Remember: the Muse sits on the forehead, so it cannot measure true frontal-midline theta.

## Debrief (one minute per group)
Show your result card. Say: your claim · your prediction · what happened · what could have fooled you (movement, eyes, drowsiness, muscle) · what you'd change.

## Troubleshooting
| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| Guided run says ear contact is poor | Fix hair / dampen ear sensors, or press **Start anyway** if you can't |
| Cue banner not visible | Scroll to the top; it stays pinned |
| Band power empty | Relative power needs ~15 s after connecting; Absolute is immediate |
| No peak in Spectrum lab | Block too short (needs ≥ 10 s after the settle time) or a poor ear contact |
| Nothing works | **Practice** mode, then sort the headband out between blocks |

**Privacy:** the app runs in your browser. Nothing is uploaded; your data exists only in this tab and in the .zip you download.
